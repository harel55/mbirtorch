# Reading list for the techniques in the `mbirtorch.hsnt` package

Grouped by technique, with the part of the code each reference explains.

## 1. The loss: Poisson transmission likelihood (NNAL)
- Lange & Carson, *EM reconstruction algorithms for emission and transmission tomography*, J. Comput. Assist. Tomogr. 8 (1984). Origin of the transmission Poisson log-likelihood `sum exp(-x) + t x`.
- Erdogan & Fessler, *Monotonic algorithms for transmission tomography*, IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 18 (1999). Surrogate / separable-quadratic treatment of this loss; background for `quadratic_update` and for the convexity of the per-pixel problem.
- Sauer & Bouman, *A local update strategy for iterative reconstruction from projections*, IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 41 (1993). Coordinate-descent lineage for transmission data.

## 2. Nonnegative factorization: model, multiplicative updates, initialization
- Lee & Seung, *Algorithms for non-negative matrix factorization*, NIPS 13 (2001). The multiplicative update generalized by `multiplicative_update`.
- Fevotte & Idier, *Algorithms for nonnegative matrix factorization with the beta-divergence*, Neural Computation 23 (2011). Majorization view of multiplicative updates for Poisson/KL losses; damping; why plain MU cannot resurrect zeros.
- O'Donoghue & Candes, *Adaptive restart for accelerated gradient schemes*, Found. Comput. Math. 15 (2015). Nesterov extrapolation with function-value restart (`multiplicative_update`).
- Boutsidis & Gallopoulos, *SVD based initialization: a head start for nonnegative matrix factorization*, Pattern Recognition 41 (2008). NNDSVD / NNDSVDa (`nndsvda`).
- Halko, Martinsson & Tropp, *Finding structure with randomness*, SIAM Review 53 (2011). Randomized SVD inside the initializer.
- Gillis, *Nonnegative Matrix Factorization*, SIAM (2020). Uniqueness, separability, minimum volume, algorithms; the gauge freedom (W, H) -> (W A, A^-1 H).
- Fu, Huang, Sidiropoulos & Ma, *Nonnegative matrix factorization for signal and data analytics: identifiability, algorithms, and applications*, IEEE Signal Process. Mag. 36 (2019).
- Collins, Dasgupta & Schapire, *A generalization of principal components analysis to the exponential family*, NIPS 14 (2001); Gordon, *Generalized^2 linear^2 models*, NIPS 15 (2002) (attribution: verify; Roy, Gordon & Thrun, JAIR 23 (2005) carries the same content). The NNAL is Poisson exponential-family PCA with a log link, X = -WH, plus nonnegativity on both factors; not a new model.
- Hong, Kolda & Duersch, *Generalized canonical polyadic tensor decomposition*, SIAM Review 62 (2020). Lists the Poisson log-link loss and solves it all at once with L-BFGS-B under bound constraints: the model class of `nnal_factorization` and the all-at-once baseline it has yet to be compared with.
- Udell, Horn, Zadeh & Boyd, *Generalized low rank models*, Found. Trends Mach. Learn. 9 (2016); Weine, Carbonetto, Irizarry & Stephens, *A new family of Poisson non-negative matrix factorization methods using the shifted log link*, arXiv:2601.05845 (2026). The exponential-family low-rank framework and the active log-link Poisson NMF line.
- Lin, *On the convergence of multiplicative update algorithms for nonnegative matrix factorization*, IEEE Trans. Neural Netw. 18 (2007). Zero entries with a negative gradient are moved off zero and every limit point is stationary: the 'boundary' mode of `_shifted`.
- Chi & Kolda, *On tensors, sparsity, and nonnegative factorizations*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 33 (2012). CP-APR; the inadmissible-zero offset for Poisson multiplicative updates and restarts from a new point: the antecedents of `_shifted` and `_reseed_dead`.
- Gillis & Glineur, *Nonnegative factorization and the maximum edge biclique problem*, CORE DP 2008/64 (2008); Takahashi & Hibi, *Global convergence of modified multiplicative updates for nonnegative matrix factorization*, Comput. Optim. Appl. 57 (2014). The epsilon-floor alternative to the shift, with convergence proofs.
- Lanteri, Theys & Richard, *Split gradient method for nonnegative matrix factorization*, EUSIPCO (2010); Badeau, Bertin & Vincent, *Stability analysis of multiplicative update algorithms and application to nonnegative matrix factorization*, IEEE Trans. Neural Netw. 21 (2010) (verify). The ratio update for any divergence and the exponent damping (`damping_factor`).
- Ang & Gillis, *Accelerating nonnegative matrix factorization algorithms using extrapolation*, Neural Computation 31 (2019). Extrapolation with a cheap error check and restart from the last accepted iterate: the skeleton of the extrapolated loop in `optimize`.
- Hien, Leplat & Gillis, *Block majorization minimization with extrapolation and application to beta-NMF*, SIAM J. Math. Data Sci. 7 (2025); Takahashi, Tanaka & Ikeda, *Majorization-minimization Bregman proximal gradient algorithms for NMF with the Kullback-Leibler divergence*, J. Optim. Theory Appl. 208 (2026). Extrapolated multiplicative/MM updates for KL/Poisson NMF with convergence proofs, which the loop here lacks.
- Biggs & Andrews, *Acceleration of iterative image restoration algorithms*, Applied Optics 36 (1997); Varadhan & Roland, *Simple and globally convergent methods for accelerating the convergence of any EM algorithm (SQUAREM)*, Scand. J. Statist. 35 (2008). Momentum on a multiplicative Poisson fixed-point map with restart, in deconvolution and EM.
- Beck & Teboulle, *Fast gradient-based algorithms for constrained total variation image denoising and deblurring problems*, IEEE Trans. Image Process. 18 (2009). Monotone FISTA: return the best iterate while the momentum sequence continues.
- Atif, Qazi & Gillis, *Improved SVD-based initialization for nonnegative matrix factorization using low-rank correction*, Pattern Recognit. Lett. 122 (2019). Replaces the NNDSVD fill heuristics; the sqrt(mean) fill of `nndsvda` is a tuning within the older family.

## 3. Projected Newton with bounds (`block_newton_step`, `_h_direction`)
- Bertsekas, *Projected Newton methods for optimization problems with simple constraints*, SIAM J. Control Optim. 20 (1982). Two-metric projection, epsilon-active set (`_ACTIVE_TOL`).
- Gafni & Bertsekas, *Two-metric projection methods for constrained optimization*, SIAM J. Control Optim. 22 (1984).
- Bertsekas, *Nonlinear Programming*, 3rd ed., Athena Scientific (2016), Sec. 3.3. Gradient projection, two-metric methods, Armijo along the projection arc.
- Kim, Sra & Dhillon, *Fast Newton-type methods for the least squares nonnegative matrix factorization problem*, SDM (2007).
- Lin, *Projected gradient methods for nonnegative matrix factorization*, Neural Computation 19 (2007).
- Hansen, Plantenga & Kolda, *Newton-based optimization for Kullback-Leibler nonnegative tensor factorizations*, Optim. Methods Softw. 30 (2015). Row-separable damped projected Newton with two-metric projection and an epsilon-active set for a Poisson-type loss: `block_newton_step` almost component for component, for the identity link.
- Gong & Zhang, *Efficient nonnegative matrix factorization via projected Newton method*, Pattern Recognition 45 (2012). Alternating NNLS with projected Newton subproblems.
- Gill, Murray, Saunders & Wright, *A practical anti-cycling procedure for linearly constrained optimization (EXPAND)*, Math. Program. 45 (1989); Facchinei, Judice & Soares, *An active set Newton algorithm for large-scale nonlinear programs with box constraints*, SIAM J. Optim. 8 (1998). The ratio-test stall on variables numerically at a bound and scale-relative active-set thresholds: what `_ACTIVE_TOL` prevents in float64.
- Smee & Roosta, *Inexact Newton-type methods for optimisation with nonnegativity constraints*, arXiv:2405.12401 (2024). Two-metric projection joined with truncated Newton-CG.

## 4. Joint truncated Newton (`joint_newton_optimize`, `_joint_newton_pcg`)
- Nocedal & Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, 2nd ed., Springer (2006). Ch. 3 line search, Ch. 4 trust regions, Ch. 5 conjugate gradients, Ch. 7 inexact Newton / Newton-CG, Sec. 10.3 Levenberg-Marquardt damping.
- Nash, *A survey of truncated-Newton methods*, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 124 (2000).
- Eisenstat & Walker, *Choosing the forcing terms in an inexact Newton method*, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 17 (1996). Adaptive CG tolerance.
- Pearlmutter, *Fast exact multiplication by the Hessian*, Neural Computation 6 (1994); Martens, *Deep learning via Hessian-free optimization*, ICML (2010). Matrix-free Hessian-vector products.
- Sorber, Van Barel & De Lathauwer, *Optimization-based algorithms for tensor decompositions*, SIAM J. Optim. 23 (2013). All-at-once inexact Gauss-Newton with matrix-free Gramian-vector products, truncated PCG and a block-Jacobi preconditioner: the architecture of `_joint_newton_pcg`, for least squares.
- Vandecappelle, Vervliet & De Lathauwer, *Inexact generalized Gauss-Newton for scaling the canonical polyadic decomposition with non-least-squares cost functions*, IEEE J. Sel. Topics Signal Process. 15 (2021). The same architecture for beta-divergences with a dogleg trust region; here the exact Hessian and bound handling are the additions.
- Ranadive & Baskaran, *An all-at-once CP decomposition method for count tensors*, IEEE HPEC (2021). All-at-once damped Gauss-Newton for Poisson nonnegative factorization beating the alternating CP-APR.
- Buchanan & Fitzgibbon, *Damped Newton algorithms for matrix factorization with missing data*, CVPR (2005); Hong & Fitzgibbon, *Secrets of matrix factorization: approximations, numerics, manifold optimization and random restarts*, ICCV (2015). Joint second-order beats alternation in narrow valleys; wall-clock evaluation; the variable-projection (Wiberg) alternative a profile-Newton baseline would follow.
- Singh, Ma, Yang & Solomonik, *Comparison of accuracy and scalability of Gauss-Newton and alternating least squares for CANDECOMP/PARAFAC decomposition*, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. (2021) (venue: verify). Gauss-Newton reaches exact decompositions where alternating least squares stalls.
- Phan, Tichavsky & Cichocki, *Low complexity damped Gauss-Newton algorithms for CANDECOMP/PARAFAC*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 34 (2013), and *Fast damped Gauss-Newton algorithm for sparse and nonnegative tensor factorization*, ICASSP (2011). All-at-once damped Gauss-Newton with nonnegativity.
- Golub & Pereyra, *The differentiation of pseudo-inverses and nonlinear least squares problems whose variables separate*, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 10 (1973); Ruhe & Wedin, *Algorithms for separable nonlinear least squares problems*, SIAM Review 22 (1980). Variable projection, and the linear rate of alternating schemes that the streamed H step inherits.

## 5. IRLS and majorization (the former `quadratic_update`, removed in the 2026-09 cleanup as dominated)
- Green, *Iteratively reweighted least squares for maximum likelihood estimation, and some robust and resistant alternatives*, JRSS B 46 (1984).
- Hunter & Lange, *A tutorial on MM algorithms*, The American Statistician 58 (2004).

## 6. Floating point (fp64 accumulation, `_ARMIJO_FLOOR`)
- Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., SIAM (2002), Ch. 2-4. Rounding of long sums; random-walk vs systematic rounding.
- Hager & Zhang, *A new conjugate gradient method with guaranteed descent and an efficient line search*, SIAM J. Optim. 16 (2005), and *Algorithm 851: CG_DESCENT*, ACM Trans. Math. Software 32 (2006). A decrease tolerance proportional to |f| because differences below the function precision are meaningless: the form of `_ARMIJO_FLOOR`.
- Berahas, Cao & Scheinberg, *Global convergence rate analysis of a generic line search algorithm with noise*, SIAM J. Optim. 31 (2021); Oztoprak, Byrd & Nocedal, *Constrained optimization in the presence of noise*, SIAM J. Optim. 33 (2023). Relaxed Armijo tests with a known noise level.
- Zhu, Byrd, Lu & Nocedal, *Algorithm 778: L-BFGS-B*, ACM Trans. Math. Software 23 (1997). The factr * epsmch stopping rule, the same c * eps * |f| form with a published constant ladder.
- Xia, Massei, Hochstenbach & Koren, *On the influence of stochastic roundoff errors and their bias on the convergence of the gradient descent method with low-precision floating-point computation*, arXiv:2202.12276 (2022); Micikevicius et al., *Mixed precision training*, ICLR (2018). Updates below half an ulp are lost under round-to-nearest, one-signed: the mechanism behind the row-length-linear noise floor and the float64 master copy.
- Connolly, Higham & Mary, *Stochastic rounding and its probabilistic backward error analysis*, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 43 (2021); Higham & Mary, *A new approach to probabilistic rounding error analysis*, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 41 (2019). When the sqrt(n) u model holds and when systematic rounding breaks it: why the /sqrt(n) floor was wrong.
- Dennis & Schnabel, *Numerical Methods for Unconstrained Optimization and Nonlinear Equations*, SIAM Classics (1996), Alg. A6.3.1 and typx scaling (trust-floor analogy: verify); More & Thuente, *Line search algorithms with guaranteed sufficient decrease*, ACM Trans. Math. Software 20 (1994). Steps too small to move the iterate, and the 'rounding errors prevent further progress' termination the floor iterates through.
- Loader, *Fast and accurate computation of binomial probabilities*, R Project technical report (2000). The Poisson unit deviance evaluated by a series near a perfect fit: the Taylor branch of `_nnal_elementwise`.

The `cox_reid` and `barndorff_nielsen` adjustments and why orthogonality matters.
- McCullagh & Tibshirani, *A simple method for the adjustment of profile likelihoods*, JRSS B 52 (1990). Adjusting the profile score by its expectation.
- Hahn & Newey, *Jackknife and analytical bias reduction for nonlinear panel models*, Econometrica 72 (2004); Kuk, *Asymptotically unbiased estimation in generalized linear models with random effects*, JRSS B 57 (1995). Bootstrap/jackknife bias corrections (`bias_corrected_spectra`, bootstrap).
- Firth, *Bias reduction of maximum likelihood estimates*, Biometrika 80 (1993).
- Chernoff, *On the distribution of the likelihood ratio*, Ann. Math. Statist. 25 (1954); Self & Liang, *Asymptotic properties of maximum likelihood estimators and likelihood ratio tests under nonstandard conditions*, JASA 82 (1987); Geyer, *On the asymptotics of constrained M-estimation*, Ann. Statist. 22 (1994); Andrews, *Estimation when a parameter is on a boundary*, Econometrica 67 (1999). The projected-normal limit of an estimate at a bound: the O(1/sqrt m) truncation term per pixel.
- Erkanli, *Laplace approximations for posterior expectations when the mode occurs at the boundary of the parameter space*, JASA 89 (1994). The interior-mode Laplace expansion behind Cox-Reid and Barndorff-Nielsen is invalid at a boundary mode: why those adjustments removed none of the truncation bias.
- Andrews, *Inconsistency of the bootstrap when a parameter is on the boundary of the parameter space*, Econometrica 68 (2000) (verify); Cavaliere, Nielsen, Pedersen & Rahbek, *Bootstrap inference on the boundary of the parameter space, with application to conditional volatility models*, J. Econometrics 227 (2022). Why the parametric bootstrap of the score underestimated the bias.
- Fernandez-Val & Weidner, *Individual and time effects in nonlinear panel models with large N, T*, J. Econometrics 192 (2016); Chen, Fernandez-Val & Weidner, *Nonlinear factor models for network and panel data*, J. Econometrics 220 (2021). Corrections for the smooth incidental-parameter term in nonlinear factor models: the O(R/K) term that remains after the truncation term is removed.
- Haberman, *Maximum likelihood estimates in exponential response models*, Ann. Statist. 5 (1977); Chen, Li & Zhang, *Joint maximum likelihood estimation for high-dimensional exploratory item factor analysis*, Psychometrika 84 (2019). Joint-ML inconsistency of a shared factor at fixed item count, the Rasch analogue of fixed K.
- Landgraf & Lee, *Generalized principal component analysis: projection of saturated model parameters*, Technometrics 62 (2020); Huang & Lee, *Debiasing sample loadings and scores in exponential family PCA for sparse count data*, arXiv:2312.13430 (2023). Incidental-parameter inconsistency and smooth debiasing in exponential-family PCA.
- Nuyts, Michel & Dupont, *Maximum-likelihood reconstruction based on a modified Poisson distribution to reduce bias in PET (NEG-ML)*, IEEE NSS/MIC (2002); Lim, Dewaraja & Fessler, *A PET reconstruction formulation that enforces non-negativity in projection space for bias reduction in Y-90 imaging*, Phys. Med. Biol. 63 (2018). Positive bias of nonnegativity-constrained Poisson ML at low counts, removed by allowing negatives where the constraint truncates noise: the logic of `unconstrained_spectra`.
- Green & Bailey, *Algorithms for non-negative matrix factorization on noisy data with negative values*, IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 72 (2024); Gemperline & Cash, *Advantages of soft versus hard constraints in self-modeling curve resolution problems*, Anal. Chem. 75 (2003). Constraint-induced offsets of the shared factor in NMF and in curve resolution.
- Ding, Li & Jordan, *Convex and semi-nonnegative matrix factorizations*, IEEE Trans. Pattern Anal. Mach. Intell. 32 (2010). Semi-NMF: the H step of `unconstrained_spectra`.
- Slawski & Hein, *Non-negative least squares for high-dimensional linear models: consistency and sparse recovery without regularization*, Electron. J. Statist. 7 (2013); Meinshausen, *Sign-constrained least squares estimation for high-dimensional regression*, Electron. J. Statist. 7 (2013). The sign constraint as an implicit regulariser: the small-pixel-count crossover where the constraint still pays.
- Ke & Wang, *Using SVD for topic modeling*, JASA 119 (2024); Arora, Ge & Moitra, *Learning topic models -- going beyond SVD*, FOCS (2012); Bing, Bunea & Wegkamp, *A fast algorithm with minimax optimal guarantees for topic models with an unknown number of topics*, Bernoulli 26 (2020); Blei, Ng & Jordan, *Latent Dirichlet allocation*, JMLR 3 (2003). - Bonhomme, Lamadon & Manresa, *Discretizing unobserved heterogeneity*, Econometrica 90 (2022) (verify). Two-step grouped fixed effects, classify then estimate: the template for support selection and the cluster gauge fix as incidental-parameter remedies.

## 11. Gauge fixing by pure pixels (`pure_pixel_gauge`)
- Fu, Huang, Sidiropoulos & Ma, *Nonnegative matrix factorization for signal and data analytics: identifiability, algorithms, and applications*, IEEE Signal Process. Mag. 36 (2019). When a nonnegative factorization is unique: separability (pure pixels) and the sufficiently-scattered condition; why the likelihood alone leaves the mixing free.
- Nascimento & Bioucas-Dias, *Vertex component analysis: a fast algorithm to unmix hyperspectral data*, IEEE Trans. Geosci. Remote Sens. 43 (2005). Endmember extraction under the pure-pixel assumption, the same assumption `pure_pixel_gauge` rests on; it takes the cluster means rather than the extreme points, which noise pushes outward.
- Arthur & Vassilvitskii, *k-means++: the advantages of careful seeding*, SODA (2007). The seeding of the cluster search.
- Lawton & Sylvestre, *Self modeling curve resolution*, Technometrics 13 (1971); Abdollahi & Tauler, *Uniqueness and rotation ambiguities in multivariate curve resolution methods*, Chemom. Intell. Lab. Syst. 108 (2011); Rajko, *Computation of the range (band boundaries) of feasible solutions and measure of the rotational ambiguity*, Anal. Chim. Acta 645 (2009); Jaumot & Tauler, *MCR-BANDS*, Chemom. Intell. Lab. Syst. 103 (2010); Neymeyr & Sawall, *On the set of solutions of the nonnegative matrix factorization problem*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 39 (2018). Rotational ambiguity and the set (area) of feasible solutions: what 'gauge' means in the chemometrics vocabulary, with band boundaries as its cost to the maps.
- Laurberg, Christensen, Plumbley, Hansen & Jensen, *Theorems on positive data: on the uniqueness of NMF*, Comput. Intell. Neurosci. (2008); Huang, Sidiropoulos & Swami, *Non-negative matrix factorization revisited: uniqueness and algorithm for symmetric decomposition*, IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 62 (2014); Fu, Huang & Sidiropoulos, *On identifiability of nonnegative matrix factorization*, IEEE Signal Process.
